Welfare & Safety

Life-threatening emergencies off campus

000

Police, fire, ambulance. On a train, bus or ferry, also press the emergency help button to alert staff when you can.

Who to contact

UNSW campus emergency 9385 6666 UNSW Campus Security. For an emergency on the Kensington or Paddington campus, call this number instead of 000. UNSW security (non-emergency) 9385 6000 For medical help at UNSW Kensington, call campus security on this number.
Grievance-related issues
@Safety_Team on Discord Grievance as in: “We have a report of a stalker.”
Operational or health-related issues
@Event_Team on Discord Operational as in: “You are kicked out of your venue.” Health as in: “Someone is bleeding hard.”

If something happens mid-event

Coming soon.

Teams are spread across Sydney, so help usually starts with a message.

What you do
  1. 1

    Fill in a grievance report if events are unfolding, or message the Welfare Lead directly for a heads-up.

  2. 2

    In the grievance form, select a location to receive help (if this is necessary).

What the welfare team does
  1. 3

    The Welfare Lead will contact you to clarify the situation, ETA and locations. If you're already in contact with another lead organiser, they'll relay information to the Welfare Lead.

  2. 4

    The Welfare Lead will then contact the volunteers in charge of the nearest area.

Come to us for help

Unless you are within a volunteer's line of sight, make your way to the agreed location rather than waiting for someone to find you, and keep following the procedure for as long as you can.

General grievance procedure

Coming soon.

If a person says something, trust them first.

  • Doesn't matter if they are a known troublemaker.
  • Doesn't matter if they like starting drama.
  • Trust them first.
Gathering information

Organisers will try to get information about the situation from the alleged victim (a description of the alleged perpetrator and their actions) and then from the alleged perpetrator, calmly:

“Hi, we've received a report that someone has felt uncomfortable due to [actions]. We aren't trying to accuse you, we just want to clear things up.”

If the response is calm and respectful, the situation is monitored and everyone moves on. If aggressive (and the action is likely true), the situation escalates.

What it leads to

Low concern / misunderstanding. Verbal boundary reminder, and a request to avoid further contact with the person who raised the report.

Moderate concern. Three strikes: warning, final warning, then removal from the event (UNSW final meet-up). The people involved are actively kept separate.

High concern / repeated offence / escalation. Removal from the entire event. Campus security contacted if at UNSW (9385 6666); life-threatening situations go straight to 000.

Supporting you

Whatever happens, the welfare team will follow up with the person who reported:

  • Explain what action is being taken.
  • Ask if you feel safe.
  • Offer ongoing support.

Thanks to Angel from MegaLAN for the original wording of this policy, adapted for SCS.
